## Cabinet styles that suit Ghanaian homes

Most kitchens we design fall into three looks. **Flat-panel modern** (handleless or slim-handle doors in matt or high-gloss) is the most requested for new villas and apartments. **Shaker and framed doors** suit family homes that want a warmer, classic finish. **Two-tone layouts** — dark base units with light wall units, or wood-effect with white — hide everyday wear on the lower doors while keeping the room bright.

Layouts follow the room: a straight run or galley for apartments, L-shape and U-shape for most houses, and an island where there is space. Many Ghanaian homes also keep an outdoor or "dirty" kitchen for heavy cooking; we can design a simpler, more robust set of units for that space alongside the main kitchen.

## Carcass and door materials for humidity and termites

The material inside the cabinet matters more than the door finish. Standard chipboard swells when it gets wet, and solid timber can attract termites. The table shows the options we normally offer.

| Part | Material options | Notes for Ghana |
|---|---|---|
| Carcass (box) | Moisture-resistant particle board (green core); marine/WBP plywood; aluminium or stainless steel frames | Plywood or metal recommended under the sink and near the cooker |
| Doors | Melamine-faced board; lacquered MDF (matt or gloss); PET/acrylic; thermofoil; solid-wood frame doors | Lacquer and PET are easy to wipe; ask about edge sealing on all doors |
| Edging | PVC or ABS edge banding, sealed on all four sides | Unsealed edges are where moisture gets in |
| Hardware | Soft-close hinges and drawer runners; stainless or coated handles | Choose corrosion-resistant finishes for coastal areas |
| Plinth / kickboard | PVC or aluminium plinth with adjustable legs | Keeps the carcass off wet floors and away from termites |

Termite risk is reduced by keeping timber off the floor, using treated or engineered boards and sealing all cut edges. Site conditions still matter, so we flag any damp walls during measurement.

## Worktops, sinks and appliance integration

We supply quartz, sintered stone, granite and laminate worktops, cut to the drawing in Foshan with sink and hob cut-outs. If you are deciding between stone types, our guide on [kitchen worktops in Ghana — quartz vs granite](/guides/kitchen-worktops-ghana-quartz-vs-granite) covers the trade-offs.

Appliances are planned into the design from the start: built-in ovens, hobs, extractor hoods, fridge housings, dishwashers and microwaves. Ghana runs on 230V / 50Hz with Type G (and Type D) sockets, so any appliances we source are specified for that supply, and the electrician gets a socket and isolator layout with the drawings. Many clients add a stabiliser or surge protection for sensitive appliances; we note this on the plan.

## FAQ

**Can you build a kitchen from my architect's drawing?**
Yes. We still check the dimensions on site before production, because walls and openings often differ from the plan.

**Which cabinet material is best for the sink area?**
Marine/WBP plywood or a metal carcass, with all edges sealed. Moisture-resistant board is fine for most other units.

**Do you supply appliances that work in Ghana?**
Yes, specified for 230V / 50Hz with the right plug type. You can also buy appliances locally and we design the housings around them.

**Do I have to order other items to make a container worthwhile?**
No. There is no minimum order. Many clients combine the kitchen with wardrobes, doors and bathrooms to share freight.

**Who installs the kitchen?**
Our Ghana team supervises installation against the approved drawing. Units arrive labelled to match the plan.
