# Building Materials in Takoradi: Port Options, Oil-City Demand and Salt-Air Specifications

Sekondi-Takoradi is Ghana's third city and its oil and gas hub, with its own seaport and a housing market shaped by energy-sector tenants and returning Western Region families. Finishing a house here raises three questions that Accra does not: whether to route imports through Takoradi port or Tema, how to specify for salt-laden sea air on exposed sites, and how to pitch finishes for a rental market that includes expatriate and corporate tenants. This guide answers each in turn.

## Takoradi port or Tema?

Takoradi has a working seaport operated by the Ghana Ports and Harbours Authority (GPHA), historically focused on bulk exports and oil and gas logistics, with a container and multi-purpose terminal developed under a private concession. Tema remains the main container gateway for the country. Which one suits a house-finishing order depends on the shipping line's service and the clearing arrangements.

| Factor | Takoradi port | Tema port |
|---|---|---|
| Container services from China | Fewer direct or feeder options; current coverage confirmed on your quote | Frequent services, most lines call |
| Clearing agents experienced in building materials | Smaller pool | Large pool; Decoropic's partners are Tema-based |
| Road leg to a Takoradi site | Short | Confirmed on your quote |
| Suitability for LCL (groupage) | Limited | Established consolidators |
| Best for | Full containers when a service is available and local clearing is arranged | Most house orders, LCL, mixed loads |

In practice many Western Region projects still clear at Tema and truck west along the coastal road. Compare the total of ocean freight plus clearing plus road delivery for both options before deciding, and confirm the shipping schedule for Takoradi at the time of order.

## Demand in the oil city

Takoradi's rental and sales market has two distinct segments:

- **Corporate and expatriate lets** tied to energy, services and logistics companies, which expect finishes close to international serviced-apartment standard: fitted kitchens with built-in appliances, good AC, reliable water, quality sanitaryware and furniture packages.
- **Owner-occupier and diaspora family homes**, often built in stages, where the priority is durability and a finish that can be maintained locally.

For a developer building for the first segment, the sample-unit approach used in Accra applies: fit one apartment fully, including furniture and lighting, and let tenants or agents see it. For the second, the Functional and Mid-Range tiers typically fit the budget, with money concentrated on doors, windows, bathrooms and the kitchen.

## Specifying for salt air

Sites within a few kilometres of the shoreline, and especially those facing the sea, experience accelerated corrosion. The specification changes are not expensive if made at the start.

| Element | Standard inland spec | Coastal Takoradi spec |
|---|---|---|
| Aluminium windows and doors | Powder-coated profile | Marine-grade pre-treatment and thicker powder coat or anodising; stainless fixings |
| External door hardware | Zinc-alloy, plated | Stainless steel (304 or better) or solid brass |
| Hinges, handles, locks | Plated steel | Stainless or marine-grade finishes |
| External lighting | Standard IP44 fittings | IP65, corrosion-resistant bodies, stainless fixings |
| Railings and gates | Mild steel, painted | Galvanised and painted, or stainless, or aluminium |
| Roof fixings and gutters | Standard | Hot-dip galvanised or stainless |
| Kitchen and wardrobe hardware | Nickel-plated | Stainless runners and hinges where offered; keep rooms ventilated |
| Sanitary fittings | Chrome on brass | Chrome on brass with regular rinsing; avoid zinc bodies |

Internal joinery is less affected but still benefits from moisture-resistant boards because humidity is high year-round.

A maintenance habit also helps: rinsing external aluminium, railings and door hardware with fresh water every few weeks removes the salt film that drives corrosion. Write this into the handover notes for tenants or facility staff, particularly on corporate lets where no one feels responsible for the exterior.

## Corporate-let finishes that tenants expect

Energy-sector and corporate tenants in Takoradi are often on short assignments and compare a unit with serviced apartments they have used elsewhere. The items that come up repeatedly in agent feedback are: a fitted kitchen with built-in hob, oven, hood and space for a large fridge; a washing machine point; strong water pressure in showers; split AC in every bedroom and the living room; good blackout curtains; a desk with power and data; security doors and window locks; and furniture that is comfortable rather than decorative. Lighting on a single warm colour temperature and a tidy consumer unit with labelled circuits also signal a well-run building. These are Mid-Range tier items in most cases, and they return in rent more reliably than decorative upgrades.

## Rain, drainage and the wet season

The Western Region receives more rainfall than Accra. Terraces, balconies and window heads need proper falls, drips and drainage so that water is thrown clear of the facade. Ground-floor rooms should have a damp-proof course and a step up from external paving. Specify external doors with a threshold and weather seal, and avoid timber external doors unless they are sheltered by a deep porch. Inside, the long wet season means bathrooms and kitchens need mechanical extraction, and wardrobes on external walls benefit from a ventilation gap behind the carcass; mould on the back of joinery is a frequent complaint in Takoradi houses that were finished without it.

## Local supply in Takoradi

The city has builders' merchants for cement, blocks, roofing, basic plumbing and electrical items, and tiles from Ghanaian factories are distributed locally. Timber is available from the region's sawmills. Imported finishing items — kitchens, wardrobes, doors, sanitaryware, lighting, furniture and aluminium systems — are usually brought in as a consolidated order, either direct to Takoradi when a service suits or via Tema.

## Planning the order for the Western Region

1. Decide the port based on the shipping schedule and clearing arrangement at order time.
2. Measure after screed and plaster; photograph the sea exposure of each elevation so finishes can be specified accordingly.
3. Group all imported items for the house into one container to spread freight.
4. Agree the road delivery and offloading plan, including access for a container truck if the box travels sealed.
5. Book installation supervision for a single visit covering kitchens, wardrobes, doors and sanitary installation.

## FAQ

**Is it cheaper to ship to Takoradi than to Tema?**
Not necessarily. Fewer services and a smaller clearing pool can offset the shorter road leg. Compare both totals at order time.

**Which items most need marine-grade specification?**
External aluminium frames, external door hardware, railings, external lighting and roof fixings.

**Can local fabricators make aluminium windows for a coastal site?**
Yes, if the profiles and coatings are specified for marine exposure and stainless fixings are used.

**How far from the sea does salt-air specification apply?**
There is no fixed line; exposure depends on wind and shelter. As a rule, sites within sight of the sea or in the prevailing wind should be treated as coastal.
