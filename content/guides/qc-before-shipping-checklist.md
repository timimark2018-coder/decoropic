# What to Check Before Ordering Custom Furniture from China

Knowing what to check before ordering custom furniture from China is the difference between a smooth handover and a container of expensive surprises. Custom pieces are made to your drawings, so mistakes are rarely caught by the factory itself. This checklist covers the stages that matter: approving samples and finishes, inspection points for each product category, the photo and video evidence to ask for, packing checks, and the warning signs of a trading company presenting itself as a factory.

## Before you pay: confirm the supplier

Start with the seller, not the sofa. Many listings on online marketplaces say "factory" but are trading companies with no production of their own. Trading companies are not a problem in themselves, as long as you know who you are dealing with and who is responsible for quality.

| Red flag | What to ask or check |
|---|---|
| "Factory" with a very wide catalogue (sofas, tiles, lighting, taps) | Ask which items they make themselves and which they buy in |
| Will not allow a visit or a live video call from the production floor | Request a scheduled video walk-through of the workshop |
| Business licence in a different name or city from the "factory" | Ask for the licence and compare company name and address |
| Prices far below other quotes for the same spec | Ask for a detailed spec sheet: material, thickness, foam density |
| Bank account in a personal name or a third party's name | Pay only to a company account matching the contract |
| No written contract or pro forma invoice with specs | Insist on drawings, specs and finishes attached to the contract |
| Pressure for full payment before production | Agree a deposit and balance tied to inspection |

## Pre-production samples and approvals

For custom work, approvals before production protect you more than inspection afterwards.

1. **Shop drawings.** Ask the factory to redraw your design with its own construction details: dimensions, joints, materials and hardware. Check every figure against your site measurements.
2. **Material samples.** Board, veneer or laminate, fabric, leather, stone, metal finish. Keep one signed sample and send one back to the factory.
3. **Finish panels.** For painted or stained items, ask for a finished panel at a usable size, photographed in daylight next to your reference.
4. **Prototype or first article.** For repeated items, such as hotel chairs or wardrobes, approve one finished piece before the full run starts.
5. **Written sign-off.** Record what was approved, the date and the reference code, so the bulk order can be checked against it.

| Approval | What it fixes | When |
|---|---|---|
| Shop drawings | Sizes, construction, hardware | Before materials are bought |
| Material and fabric samples | Colour, texture, quality | Before cutting |
| Finish panel | Paint colour, sheen, stain tone | Before finishing starts |
| Prototype | Comfort, proportions, assembly | Before bulk production |
| Pre-shipment inspection | Quantity, workmanship, packing | Before balance payment |

## Inspection points by category

Ask for inspection when production is complete and at least most of the order is packed, with the remainder open for checking. The table lists the points that most often go wrong.

| Category | Key checks |
|---|---|
| Kitchen and wardrobe cabinets | Carcass dimensions against drawings; edge-banding fully sealed (moisture protection); door gaps even; hinges and runners soft-close and adjusted; worktop cut-outs in the right place; hardware bags counted and labelled |
| Upholstery (sofas, beds, chairs) | Frame solid with no movement; seat height and depth to spec; foam density as agreed; fabric matches approved swatch; seams straight; no stains; legs secure |
| Casegoods (desks, bedside tables) | Veneer or laminate free of bubbles and chips; drawers run smoothly; colour consistent across pieces; levelling feet fitted |
| Sanitaryware | Glaze free of cracks, pinholes and crazing; outlet and fixing positions to spec; matching batch colour; tap and flush parts included; WC pan and cistern pairs matched |
| Doors and frames | Door and frame sizes against the opening schedule; swing direction; lock and hinge preparation; finish free of runs; frames packed with doors as sets |
| Lighting | Every fitting tested on power; voltage suitable for Ghana's mains supply; colour temperature as specified; drivers included; spare lamps; mounting kits |
| Stone and tiles | Batch and shade consistency; thickness; chipped edges; quantity including wastage allowance |

Inspect a sample from each item line, not only the easy cartons at the front. For custom items with few pieces, inspect them all.

## Photo and video evidence

If you cannot attend in person, evidence must be specific enough to rely on. Ask for:

- **Wide shot then close-up** of each item, with its item code visible on a label.
- **Measurement photos** with a tape measure readable on the key dimensions.
- **Daylight colour comparison** next to the approved sample.
- **Function videos**: doors opening, drawers running, recliners moving, lights switched on.
- **Defect photos** with a note of how each will be fixed, and a second photo after repair.
- **Date and order reference** in the photo set, so old photos cannot be reused.

Store originals, not just screenshots in a chat. If something goes wrong later, these files are your evidence for the supplier and the insurer.

## Packing checks

Good furniture can still arrive damaged. Before the cartons are sealed, check:

| Check | Pass standard |
|---|---|
| Inner protection | Film, foam and corner guards on all exposed edges |
| Outer packing | Five-ply cartons or wooden crates for heavy, fragile or stone items |
| Labelling | Item code, room, box number "x of y", "this way up" |
| Hardware | Fixing bags taped inside the right carton and listed |
| Moisture | Dry packing; desiccant planned for the container |
| Count | Carton count matches the packing list |
| Loading | Photos of the container door, the loaded cargo and the seal number |

## Payment linked to quality

Tie money to milestones. A common structure is a deposit to start production and the balance after a satisfactory pre-shipment inspection. Letters of credit can name an inspection certificate as one of the documents required before payment. Whatever structure you use, write the specs, approved samples and inspection standard into the contract.

Also agree in writing what happens if the inspection fails: who pays for re-work, how long the factory has to correct it, and whether a second inspection is needed. Keep the inspection report with the order file. If a problem appears after delivery in Ghana, you can then show whether the defect was present before loading or happened in transit, which decides whether you claim against the supplier or the cargo insurer.

## FAQ

**Do I need a sample before ordering custom furniture?**
For anything made to your drawings, yes: at least material and finish samples, and a prototype for repeated items.

**Who should do the inspection?**
Someone independent of the production line, such as your buying agent, a third-party inspector or your supplier's own warehouse team, working to a written checklist.

**What if defects are found?**
Agree the fix, the new completion date and a re-inspection before balance payment. Keep before-and-after photos.

**How can I tell a real factory from a trading company?**
Ask for a live video walk-through of the workshop, check the business licence name and address, and see whether the catalogue is narrow enough to be made in one place.

**Is inspection worth it for a small order?**
Yes. Correcting a defect in Foshan is far easier than replacing an item after shipping, clearance and delivery in Ghana.
