# Shipping a Container of House Finishing Materials to Ghana: How a 40HQ Is Packed, and LCL vs FCL

A single 40-foot high-cube container can carry the finishing materials for a whole villa or several apartments: kitchens, wardrobes, doors, sanitaryware, lighting, furniture and hardware. Packing it well is the difference between a smooth installation in Ghana and weeks of chasing missing cartons. This guide explains what a 40HQ holds, how it is loaded in order of unloading, how mixed categories are consolidated from different factories, and when a smaller LCL (groupage) shipment makes more sense than a full container.

## Container sizes in plain terms

| Container | Internal length × width × height (approx.) | Usable volume (approx.) | Typical house-finishing use |
|---|---|---|---|
| 20 ft standard | 5.9 m × 2.35 m × 2.39 m | about 33 m³ | One apartment kitchen-and-wardrobe package, or doors and sanitaryware for a small building |
| 40 ft standard | 12.0 m × 2.35 m × 2.39 m | about 67 m³ | A large villa's joinery and bathrooms |
| 40 ft high cube (40HQ) | 12.0 m × 2.35 m × 2.69 m | about 76 m³ | Full villa finishing, or 4–8 apartments' kitchens, wardrobes, doors and sanitaryware |

Dimensions vary slightly by container owner; the figures above are the common published ranges. Weight limits also apply, though house-finishing loads are usually volume-limited rather than weight-limited unless stone worktops or tiles are included.

## What a full-house load looks like

A typical four-bedroom villa order consolidated by Decoropic might include the categories below. Quantities are illustrative of a layout, not a quotation.

| Category | Form in the container | Packing |
|---|---|---|
| Kitchen cabinets | Flat-pack carcasses, doors in separate cartons, worktops crated | Cartons labelled by cabinet code; worktops in timber crates, upright |
| Wardrobes | Flat-pack, by bedroom | Cartons labelled by room |
| Interior and security doors | Leaves and frames palletised or crated | Corner protection; steel doors crated |
| Sanitaryware | WCs, basins, shower trays on pallets with foam | Fragile; loaded last-in, top layer never used |
| Shower enclosures, mirrors | Glass in timber crates | Upright, strapped to container wall |
| Mixers, hardware, accessories | Cartons | Grouped by bathroom; small items boxed together |
| Lighting | Cartons | Grouped by room; drivers and lamps included |
| Furniture | Flat-pack where possible; sofas and beds assembled or part-assembled | Blanket-wrapped and strapped |
| Aluminium windows and doors | Frames with glass, crated or racked | Where part of the order; often a separate container on large jobs |

## Loading in reverse order

The container is loaded so that what is needed first on site comes out first:

1. **Deep in the container (loaded first, unloaded last):** furniture and lighting, which are installed at the end.
2. **Middle:** wardrobes and kitchen cabinets.
3. **Near the doors (loaded last, unloaded first):** doors and frames, sanitaryware and shower enclosures, because frames go in before joinery and bathrooms are often tiled first.

Heavy, dense items (worktops, steel doors, sanitaryware pallets) sit low and against the walls; light cartons fill the upper space. Everything is strapped or braced so nothing shifts during the voyage and the road leg in Ghana. A packing list with carton numbers, contents, room and weight is issued with photographs of each loading stage.

## Consolidating from several factories

House-finishing products come from different specialised factories — kitchen, door, sanitary, lighting. Consolidation means collecting from each, checking quantities and damage, repacking where needed and loading into one container at a single point. The practical benefits for a Ghana buyer:

- One bill of lading, one clearing process at Tema, one delivery
- Shared freight across all categories, so small items (handles, lights) ride almost free
- A single packing list and one point of responsibility for shortages
- No minimum per category, so a project can take ten doors and one kitchen in the same box

## LCL vs FCL: when a full container is not the answer

| Factor | LCL (less than container load / groupage) | FCL (full container) |
|---|---|---|
| Suits | One kitchen, a few doors, a sanitaryware set, samples | A whole house or more |
| Cost logic | Pay per cubic metre; cost per m³ higher than FCL but no empty space | Flat container cost; cheaper per m³ when the box is reasonably full |
| Handling | Goods are packed and unpacked at consolidation warehouses at both ends; more touches | Loaded once at origin, opened at destination |
| Damage risk | Higher for fragile sanitaryware and glass | Lower; packing is controlled |
| Transit time | Often longer because the groupage box waits to fill and is devanned at Tema | Direct |
| Clearing at Tema | Through the consolidator's agent; charges per shipment | Through your agent; container deposit and empty return apply |

As a working guide, a single apartment's kitchen and wardrobes is usually LCL territory; two or three apartments or one full villa justify a 20 ft or 40HQ. If the load would leave a 40HQ more than a third empty, it can be worth adding items that will be needed later (spare doors, lighting for the next phase) rather than paying for air.

## On arrival: unpacking and site storage

Book the delivery for a day when the site has labour and a dry, lockable store. Unpack against the packing list, photograph any damage before moving the item, and stack cartons by room. Keep sanitaryware and glass crated until the day they are installed. Return the empty container within the free period to avoid detention charges; the free period is confirmed on your quote.

## FAQ

**Can tiles go in the same container?**
Yes, but tiles are heavy and often cheaper to buy from Ghanaian factories. Include them only when a specific imported tile is required.

**How full should the container be before it is worth shipping?**
If the load fills at least two-thirds of the box, FCL usually beats LCL. Below that, compare both.

**Who is responsible if something arrives broken?**
Marine insurance covers transit damage when the goods were packed properly; report with photographs on the day of unpacking. Ask for the policy terms before shipment.

**Can two houses share a container?**
Yes. Cartons are labelled by house and room, and the packing list separates them.
