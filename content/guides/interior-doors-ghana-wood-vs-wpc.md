# Interior Doors in Ghana: Wood vs WPC vs Steel Security Doors

Doors are bought by the dozen on a Ghanaian house project, and the wrong choice is multiplied by the dozen. Termites, humidity, poor frames and cheap locks account for most door complaints in Accra, Kumasi and Takoradi. This guide compares solid timber, engineered and flush doors, WPC (wood-plastic composite) doors and steel security doors, explains how to specify frames and hardware so the leaf stays square, and sets out where local joinery is the better choice and where importing a matched set makes sense.

## The door types in use

| Door type | Construction | Termite resistance | Humidity resistance | Typical use in Ghana |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Solid hardwood (odum, mahogany, sapele) | Panelled or planked solid timber | Good for odum; variable otherwise | Good if kiln-dried; moves if not | Main entrance, feature internal doors |
| Engineered timber / veneered | Timber or composite core with veneer skins | Low unless treated | Moderate; MR cores better | Internal doors in finished homes |
| Flush hollow-core | Honeycomb core, hardboard or MDF skins | Low | Low; swells at the bottom edge | Budget internal doors |
| WPC (wood-plastic composite) | Extruded WPC frame and skins, often with PVC film or paint finish | Excellent | Excellent; suitable for bathrooms | Internal doors, bathrooms, wet-area doors |
| Steel security door | Pressed steel skins, reinforced frame, multi-point lock | Excellent | Good if coated; watch coastal sites | Entrance and back doors, apartment front doors |
| Aluminium framed glass | Aluminium frame, glass panels | Excellent | Excellent | Patio, balcony, internal partitions |

## Termites and humidity: the two tests

Any door in Ghana should pass two questions. Will termites eat it, and will it swell?

- **Termites.** Hollow-core and standard MDF doors on ground floors are the most common casualties. Treated frames, a gap from the floor, and termite control at the slab stage reduce risk, but the surest route for ground-floor and bathroom doors is a WPC or steel leaf and a WPC, aluminium or treated hardwood frame.
- **Humidity.** Timber doors should be kiln-dried and sealed on all six faces before hanging — including top and bottom edges, which are usually left bare and are where water enters. WPC does not absorb water, so bathroom and kitchen doors in WPC avoid the swollen-bottom-edge problem that plagues flush doors.

## Frames, architraves and the gap

A good leaf in a bad frame still sticks. Frame rules that save callbacks:

- Frame material should match the leaf's exposure: WPC frame with WPC door, hardwood frame with hardwood door, steel frame with steel door.
- Fix frames to the block wall with proper anchors at hinge positions; foam alone is not enough.
- Allow a 3 mm gap at sides and top and 8–10 mm at the bottom over the finished floor, more if a carpet or thick mat is planned.
- Architraves should cover the plaster junction; in bathrooms use WPC or PVC architraves.
- Confirm the finished floor level before frames are set, so door bottoms do not need trimming after tiling.

## Hardware that lasts

| Item | Minimum spec for Ghana | Upgrade |
|---|---|---|
| Hinges | Stainless or solid brass ball-bearing, 3 per door | 4 for doors over 2,100 mm or heavy leaves |
| Internal lock | Tubular latch or mortice lock with brass or stainless bolt | Magnetic latch for quiet closing |
| Bathroom lock | Privacy set with emergency release | Stainless finish for wet rooms |
| Entrance lock | Multi-point steel-door lock or heavy mortice deadlock | Smart lock with mechanical override; keep a key |
| Handles | Zinc-alloy with good plating is acceptable inside | Stainless or brass on coastal sites and entrances |
| Door stops and closers | Floor or wall stops on every door | Closers on bathroom and kitchen doors in hotels |

## Steel security doors

Pressed-steel entrance doors with multi-point locks are widely used on Ghanaian apartments and villas. Points to check:

- Steel thickness of the skins and the frame; thin skins dent and look cheap.
- Coating: powder coat or baked paint over a primer; on coastal sites specify extra coating or choose a stainless-faced option.
- Locking: multi-point bolts, anti-drill cylinder, and a mechanical key even if a fingerprint or code lock is fitted.
- Fire and smoke seals where the door separates a flat from a common corridor.
- Fit: steel frames are rigid; the opening must be formed accurately or packed with care.

## Local vs imported

| Route | Strengths | Weaknesses |
|---|---|---|
| Local joiner (hardwood doors and frames) | Beautiful odum and mahogany work; adjustable on site; supports local craft | Kiln-dried stock scarce; finish quality varies; lead time depends on the joiner |
| Local supply of imported stock doors | Immediate availability from dealers | Limited sizes and finishes; mixed quality |
| Imported matched set (WPC, engineered, steel) | Consistent finish across the house; sized to each opening; hardware supplied with the door | Must be measured correctly; production and shipping time; freight shared with other items |

A sensible combination on many Ghanaian houses is a locally made solid hardwood entrance door for character, WPC doors throughout the interior and bathrooms for durability, and steel doors on back and service entrances.

## Finishes and how they age

Door finish decides how the house looks after three years, not three weeks. Factory paint and PVC film finishes on WPC and engineered doors resist fingerprints and wiping well, and the colour is consistent from door to door. Site-applied varnish on hardwood looks good at first but needs recoating as sun and humidity work on it, especially on doors near windows. Veneered doors need a hard-wearing lacquer and careful handling before hanging, since a chipped veneer edge cannot be touched up invisibly. For rental units and hotels, choose finishes that can be wiped with ordinary cleaners and touched up with a small kit left on site.

## Acoustic and privacy considerations

Hollow-core doors do little for sound. Where bedrooms sit beside a living area, or in guest houses where corridor noise matters, a solid-core engineered door or a thicker WPC leaf with a drop seal and a proper stop makes a noticeable difference. Bathroom doors should close against a stop with a seal so that fan noise and humidity stay inside.

## Measuring openings for an import order

Measure each opening at three points for width and two for height after plaster and with the floor level confirmed. Record swing direction (left or right hand, push or pull), wall thickness for the frame depth, and which doors need locks versus latches. Photograph every opening with a number chalked on the wall. Order a small number of spare leaves in the most common size.

## FAQ

**Are WPC doors strong enough for bedrooms?**
Yes for internal use. Choose a thicker leaf and reinforced lock block where a heavy mortice lock is required.

**Can WPC doors be painted?**
Many are supplied with a PVC film or factory paint finish. Some can be repainted; check the supplier's guidance.

**Do steel doors rust in Accra?**
Properly coated steel doors perform well inland and in most of Accra. Within sight of the sea, specify an upgraded coating or stainless facing.

**Should I buy doors before the house is plastered?**
No. Measure after plaster and with the floor level fixed, otherwise frames will not fit.
