# Guest House Renovation in Ghana: Refreshing 10–40 Rooms Without Closing

Most guest houses and small hotels in Ghana cannot afford to close for three months. Rooms must keep earning while the property is brought up to the standard that online reviews and corporate bookings now demand. This guide sets out how to refresh a 10–40 room property in phases: what to change first, how to build a repeatable room kit, how to keep noise and dust away from paying guests, and how to buy finishes so that every phase matches the last.

## Decide what the refresh is for

Before specifying anything, write down the commercial reason for the project. The answer changes the scope.

| Objective | Typical scope | Rooms taken out at once |
|---|---|---|
| Fix bad reviews (bathrooms, smell, lighting) | Bathrooms, extractor fans, LED lighting, mattresses | 2–4 |
| Move up a price band | Full room kit: floor, wall finish, joinery, bathroom, AC | 4–8 |
| Win corporate or NGO contracts | Desk, power points, reliable Wi-Fi, blackout curtains, safe | 4–8 |
| Prepare for sale or a lease | Public areas, facade, lobby, restaurant, plus rooms | Phased by wing |

A 24-room property that keeps 18 rooms trading can usually complete a full refresh in four to six phases over several months, with each phase lasting a few weeks. The exact programme depends on how much wet work (tiling, plumbing) is involved.

## Build one room kit and repeat it

The single most useful decision in a small hotel renovation is to design one standard room and repeat it. A kit list for a mid-range Ghanaian guest room typically includes:

- Floor: porcelain tile (locally available from Ghanaian factories) or SPC vinyl plank for upper floors
- Walls: washable emulsion, one feature wall in wallpaper or timber-look panel behind the bed
- Joinery: headboard panel, wardrobe or open hanging unit, luggage bench, desk and mirror, bedside units
- Bathroom: wall-hung or close-coupled WC, basin with vanity unit, shower screen, thermostatic or single-lever mixer, extractor fan
- Doors: solid-core or WPC door with a hotel-grade lock (card or mechanical)
- Lighting: warm-white LED downlights, bedside reading lights, bathroom mirror light, all on 230 V drivers
- Services: split-unit AC, enough double sockets at desk and bedside, USB charging if budget allows
- Soft goods: blackout curtain plus sheer, mattress, linen

Order the whole kit for all rooms at once, even if installation is phased. This is the only way to guarantee that room 3 and room 23 have the same tap, the same tile batch and the same door handle. Hold the later phases' stock in a locked store on site.

## Phasing while trading

| Phase | Work | Guest impact control |
|---|---|---|
| 0 — Survey and sample room | Measure all rooms, note differences, fit one sample room completely | Use the sample room to confirm the kit before bulk ordering |
| 1 — Services and noisy work | Plumbing risers, electrical rewiring, tile removal in the first block | Schedule 9 am–5 pm only; seal corridors with plastic sheeting; move guests to the far wing |
| 2 — Wet finishes | Tiling, waterproofing, screeds | Dust control, daily clean of shared corridors |
| 3 — Joinery and doors | Wardrobes, headboards, vanity units, doors and locks | Quieter work; can run while adjacent rooms are let |
| 4 — Lighting, AC, soft goods | Fit-off, test, snag | One day per room |
| 5 — Public areas | Lobby, restaurant, external lighting, signage | Night or off-season work |

Keep a written snag list per room and do not release a room to the front desk until it is signed off. Guests notice a missing toilet-roll holder more than they notice a new tile.

## Bathrooms: the part that drives reviews

Bathrooms are where most guest house budgets should go. Points that matter in Ghana specifically:

- **Water pressure and storage.** Many properties run on a roof tank and a pump. Choose mixers and showers rated for low pressure, or confirm the pump delivers the pressure the shower needs.
- **Waterproofing.** Apply a liquid membrane to the floor and 300 mm up the walls before tiling. Leaks into the room below are the most expensive defect to fix later.
- **Ventilation.** An extractor fan on the light switch, ducted to outside, reduces mould and smell. A window alone is not enough in the wet season.
- **Fittings that survive.** Brass-bodied taps with ceramic cartridges, stainless or brass wastes, and a shower screen rather than a curtain. Avoid zinc-alloy taps that corrode within two years.
- **Tiles.** Large-format porcelain on walls reduces grout lines and cleaning time; the local factories now produce suitable sizes, so tiles rarely need importing.

## Lighting and power

Replace fluorescent fittings and mixed-colour bulbs with LED on a single colour temperature (3000 K is a safe hotel choice). Put reading lights at each bedside, a mirror light in the bathroom and a two-way switch at the bed. Every fitting and driver must be rated for 230 V 50 Hz and installed to local wiring practice by a licensed electrician; see our lighting import guide for conformity details.

## Procurement: local vs imported, and when to consolidate

Tiles, cement, paint, timber and basic plumbing are best bought locally. Joinery, doors, sanitaryware, hotel locks, mixers and lighting are where importing a consolidated order makes sense for a 10–40 room property, because the per-room cost of quality falls when freight is spread across all rooms and the finish is identical throughout. The trade-off is lead time: allow the production and shipping period in the programme (confirmed on your quote), and order before phase 1 starts so that stock is on site when the joinery phase begins.

Payment is normally by T/T in stages or by L/C for larger orders. Keep the supply contract and the installation contract separate so that each party is responsible for its own work.

## Budget priorities when money is tight

Few guest houses can do everything in one round. If the budget forces choices, the order that usually gives the best return on reviews and rate is: bathrooms first, then mattresses and linen, then lighting and power points, then the bedroom joinery and doors, and finally floors and public areas. A clean, well-lit bathroom with strong water and a quiet, cool room with a working AC and sockets by the bed will lift reviews more than a new lobby. Public-area work can be pushed to the off-season, when occupancy is lowest and noise matters less.

## Staff, housekeeping and the handover

Renovated rooms fail quickly if housekeeping is not briefed. Before the first refreshed rooms go back on sale, walk the housekeeping team through the new fittings: how soft-close hinges work, which cleaners damage quartz vanities or lacquered doors, how the extractor fan is switched, and how to reset a card lock. Leave a laminated room sheet in the housekeeping store with the product codes for every replaceable item — lamp, driver, tap cartridge, hinge, runner — so a replacement can be ordered by code rather than by description. A small spares box per floor saves weeks of a room being out of service for a single part.

## FAQ

**How many rooms should be out of service at once?**
For a property under 40 rooms, four to eight is typical. Fewer than four makes the programme very long; more than eight hurts cash flow.

**Should we close completely instead?**
Only if occupancy is already very low or the building needs structural work. Phasing costs more in management time but keeps revenue.

**Do we need an architect or interior designer?**
For a repeat-room refresh, a good designer producing one room drawing set and a finishes schedule is enough. Structural changes need an engineer.

**What is the most common mistake?**
Buying finishes phase by phase from different suppliers, so later rooms do not match earlier ones.
