# Renovating a House in Ghana While Abroad: Remote Approvals, Video Walk-Throughs and Payment Milestones

Many Ghanaian homes are built or renovated by owners living in London, New York, Toronto or Dubai, managing the work through a relative or a contractor by phone. The familiar problems — money sent, work not done, finishes substituted, nobody accountable — are usually management failures rather than bad luck. This guide sets out a working system for remote control: who should be on the ground, how approvals and changes are recorded, how to run a video walk-through that actually verifies progress, and how to tie every payment to evidence.

## The people you need on the ground

| Role | What they do | What to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Site lead (relative, friend or paid project manager) | Daily eyes; receives deliveries; sends photographs; holds keys | Making them responsible for technical decisions they are not qualified for |
| Contractor or trades | Builds and installs | Paying them for stages that have not been verified |
| Independent checker (designer, engineer or supplier's supervisor) | Confirms work matches drawings before stage payments | Relying only on the contractor's own photographs |
| Supplier with a local office | Delivers, supervises installation of its products | Suppliers with no presence in Ghana who disappear after delivery |

Having two independent sources of information — a site lead and a checker — is what makes remote management work. Each reports to you separately, and discrepancies are discussed before money moves.

## Set the scope in writing before any money moves

A renovation run from abroad needs a written scope that a stranger could follow:

- Drawings, even simple ones, with room names and dimensions
- A finishes schedule: floor, wall, ceiling, doors, windows, joinery, sanitaryware, lighting, per room
- A kit list for imported items with codes matching the carton labels
- A programme with stages and dates
- A payment schedule linked to the stages
- A change procedure: any change is proposed in writing with a cost and time effect, and approved in writing before it is done

Share these as a single folder (cloud drive) that everyone on the project can read. The folder becomes the single source of truth.

## Video walk-throughs that verify, not just show

A live video call once a week, at a fixed time, with a fixed route through the house, is more useful than a stream of random photographs. A good walk-through:

1. Starts outside with the date visible (a newspaper, a phone screen), then enters through the main door and follows the same route every week.
2. Pauses in each room for a slow 360-degree pan, then close-ups of the items on that week's checklist.
3. Checks the specifics: tile batch codes on cartons, door labels, the model number on a mixer, the colour on a paint tin, the width of a wardrobe against the drawing with a tape measure held to the camera.
4. Is recorded (screen recording) and saved to the project folder with the date.
5. Ends with the site lead reading out next week's planned work.

Ask for measurements to be called out on camera, not just shown. A tape held against a wall tells you more than a wide shot ever will.

## Payment milestones tied to evidence

| Stage | Evidence required before release | Typical trigger |
|---|---|---|
| Mobilisation / deposit | Signed scope, programme and payment schedule | Contract signing |
| Demolition and services | Walk-through showing pipework and conduit before they are covered; photographs of every wall | Before plaster or screed |
| Wet finishes | Waterproofing membrane photographed before tiling; tile batch and layout confirmed | Before tiling starts |
| Joinery delivery | Carton count against packing list; damage photographs | Delivery day |
| Joinery and doors installed | Walk-through with tape measure; doors swing and latch; drawers run | Checker's sign-off |
| Lighting and sanitary fit-off | Every light switched on camera; every tap run; WC flushed | Checker's sign-off |
| Final | Snag list closed; keys, spares and manuals handed to the site lead | Hand-over |

Keep the final payment meaningful — enough that the contractor returns for snags. Pay suppliers and contractors separately so that each is paid for its own work; never route supplier payments through the contractor.

## Imported items: how the remote owner stays in control

Imported kitchens, wardrobes, doors and sanitaryware are easier to control from abroad than site-built work, because the specification is fixed at the drawing stage and the goods arrive labelled. The owner approves the drawings and the finish samples (photographs or physical samples posted abroad), the factory produces to those drawings, and the installer fits what arrives. Substitution is far less likely than with items bought piecemeal in Accra by a third party. Measurement is the critical step: either the supplier measures on site or the site lead measures to the supplier's checklist with photographs.

## Common failure points and fixes

| Failure | Fix |
|---|---|
| Money sent for materials, receipts never seen | Supplier invoices paid directly by the owner; site lead only handles small cash |
| Finishes substituted with cheaper ones | Finishes schedule with photographs; batch codes checked on camera |
| Work covered up before checking | Services and waterproofing stages have a hold point; no cover-up without a walk-through |
| Programme slips without warning | Weekly call with next week's plan on record |
| Disputes about what was agreed | Everything in the shared folder; changes approved in writing |
| No one to fix defects after hand-over | Retention held; supplier with a Ghana office for installed products |

## Tools that make remote management easier

None of this needs special software. A shared cloud folder with sub-folders per stage, a WhatsApp group for daily traffic with a rule that decisions are copied into the folder, a simple spreadsheet for the payment schedule with a column for the evidence file name, and a calendar reminder for the weekly call cover most projects. For larger builds, a free project-management board with one card per room and per stage keeps the snag list visible to everyone. Time zones matter: fix the weekly call at a time that suits Ghana working hours and the site lead's availability, even if it is early morning in North America.

## Visits: making the most of time on the ground

When you do fly in, prepare the visit as a checklist rather than a tour. Measure and photograph every room, sign off samples physically, meet the checker and the contractor together, pay only against evidence you have now seen, and agree the next two milestones in writing before leaving. Buy nothing on impulse in Accra that is not on the finishes schedule; unplanned purchases are the usual way a project drifts off specification.

## FAQ

**Should I use a relative as site lead?**
A trusted relative is fine for daily presence and deliveries, but pair them with an independent checker for technical sign-off.

**How often should I visit?**
At minimum at the start (scope and measurement) and before final payment. Mid-project visits at the joinery stage are valuable.

**What if the contractor refuses weekly video calls?**
That is a warning sign. Make the weekly call a condition of the contract.

**Can approvals be done on WhatsApp?**
Yes, provided each approval is also saved to the shared folder with the drawing or photograph it refers to.
