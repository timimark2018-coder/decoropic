# Cost to Finish a 4 Bedroom House in Ghana: How the Budget Breaks Down

The cost to finish a 4 bedroom house in Ghana is not one number. It is a stack of eight or nine category budgets, each of which moves independently depending on the specification you choose, where the material is made, and how it reaches the site. This guide explains how finishing budgets are structured, which categories carry the most weight, and how to build a figure you can defend before you place a single order. It does not quote totals; it shows you how to arrive at yours.

## What "finishing" covers and what it does not

Finishing starts when the structure is weather-tight: roof on, external walls rendered, floor slabs cured, first-fix plumbing and electrical conduits in the walls. Everything after that point is finishing.

A typical finishing scope for a detached 4 bedroom house in Accra, Kumasi or Tema includes:

- Floor and wall tiles, skirting, and wet-area waterproofing
- Internal doors, frames, architraves and ironmongery
- External doors and aluminium or uPVC windows
- Kitchen cabinets, worktops, sink and tap
- Wardrobes and built-in storage
- Sanitaryware (WCs, basins, showers, baths) and brassware
- Lighting fittings, switches, sockets and distribution board
- Ceilings (POP or gypsum board), paint and decorative finishes
- Loose furniture, curtains and soft furnishings (optional, often a separate budget)

Finishing does not include structural work, septic or soakaway, external paving, boundary walls, water storage tanks or the generator. Keep those on a separate line so your finishing number stays honest.

## The nine categories and their typical share of the budget

The table below shows where money tends to go. The shares are indicative ranges for a conventional 4 bedroom house with all bedrooms en suite; the exact split for your project depends on tier and design. Absolute values are not given here because they change with exchange rate, freight and specification. Use the /estimator to generate a figure for your floor area and tier.

| Category | Main cost drivers |
| --- | --- |
| Tiles and flooring | Format size, porcelain vs ceramic, wet-area coverage, laying pattern |
| Kitchen cabinets and worktop | Linear metres, carcass material, door finish, stone vs laminate top |
| Wardrobes and joinery | Number of rooms, height to ceiling, sliding vs hinged, internal fittings |
| Doors and windows | Aluminium profile series, glass spec, number of openings, security grilles |
| Sanitaryware and brassware | Number of bathrooms, wall-hung vs floor-standing, concealed cisterns |
| Lighting and electrical fittings | Fitting count, LED driver quality, outdoor IP-rated fittings, smart controls |
| Ceilings and paint | Gypsum vs POP, coffered designs, paint grade, number of coats |
| Loose furniture | Solid wood vs veneer, upholstery grade, imported vs local |
| Installation labour | Trade rates in your city, supervision, waste and rework |

Two things are consistently true. Kitchens and wardrobes together are usually the largest single joinery spend, and bathrooms are where the Mid-Range and Premium tiers pull away from Functional, because the fittings multiply by the number of en suites.

## Three tiers: Functional, Mid-Range, Premium

Decoropic designs to three tiers so that a client can hold the floor plan constant and move the budget up or down.

| Tier | Who it suits | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| Functional | Rental property, first family home, tight completion date | Standard-format tiles, laminate or melamine joinery, basic aluminium windows, floor-standing sanitaryware, surface-mounted lighting |
| Mid-Range | Owner-occupied family home, diaspora self-build | Larger-format porcelain, lacquer or PET door fronts, quartz worktops, concealed cisterns, recessed LED, better ironmongery |
| Premium | Executive villa, show home, hotel-standard finish | Slab-format or natural stone, custom veneer or solid-wood joinery, thermally improved aluminium, wall-hung sanitaryware, layered lighting with dimming, loose furniture to a design scheme |

Mixing tiers is normal. Many clients go Mid-Range on bedrooms and Premium on the kitchen, living room and master suite, because those are the rooms visitors see.

## Local versus imported: where each category now sits

Ghana's tile industry has grown. Factories such as Twyford and Keda produce in Ghana, so standard floor and wall tiles are now widely available locally and the case for importing them has narrowed to special formats and finishes. Budget planning should reflect this: buy standard tiles locally, and spend your import effort on categories where Ghana does not yet have depth.

| Category | Buy locally | Import from China |
|---|---|---|
| Standard tiles 600×600 and smaller | Yes, in most cases | Only for specific designs |
| Large-format slabs, marble-look, outdoor 20 mm | Limited | Yes |
| Kitchen cabinets, wardrobes | Limited custom capacity | Yes, made to your measurements |
| Aluminium and uPVC doors and windows | Fabricators exist; profile quality varies | Yes, for system profiles and glazing specs |
| Sanitaryware and brassware | Basic ranges | Yes, for full matched sets and hardware |
| Lighting | Basic fittings | Yes, for decorative and architectural ranges |
| Furniture | Some local production | Yes, for coordinated sets and hotel FF&E |
| Cement, sand, blocks, POP, paint | Yes | No |

## Hidden costs that break finishing budgets

Most overruns come from items that were never in the first estimate.

- **Freight and clearance.** CIF Tema price is not landed price. Duties, VAT and levies are applied at the port, and clearing agent fees, port handling and inland haulage sit on top. See the Tema clearing guide for the levy names.
- **Wastage.** Tiles need a 10 to 15 percent allowance for cuts and breakage; more for diagonal or herringbone patterns.
- **Measurement errors.** A kitchen made 40 mm too long for the wall is a reorder, not an adjustment. Site measurement after plastering, not from drawings, prevents this.
- **Electrical and plumbing second fix.** Switch plates, sockets, isolators, traps, angle valves and flexible connectors are small items that add up.
- **Scope creep.** Adding a feature wall, a coffered ceiling or an extra outdoor kitchen late in the project costs more than it would have at design stage.
- **Exchange rate movement.** If you pay in cedis for goods priced in US dollars, build in a margin for the period between quotation and payment.

## How to build your own finishing budget in five steps

1. Fix the floor plan and count the openings: rooms, doors, windows, bathrooms, kitchen run length in metres.
2. Pick a tier for each category, not one tier for the whole house.
3. Decide what will be bought locally and what will be imported, using the table above.
4. Run the numbers through the /estimator to get a category-by-category figure for your floor area.
5. Add freight, clearance, installation labour and a contingency of at least 10 percent as separate lines.

## FAQ

**Can you give me a total cost to finish a 4 bedroom house in Ghana?**
Not responsibly, without a floor plan and a tier. Two 4 bedroom houses with the same footprint can differ by a wide margin depending on the kitchen, the bathrooms and the windows. Use the estimator for a figure based on your own inputs.

**Is it cheaper to import everything from China?**
No. Standard tiles, cement, blocks, POP and paint are better bought locally. Importing pays off for custom joinery, doors and windows, sanitaryware sets, lighting and furniture, where local supply is thin or quality is inconsistent.

**How much contingency should I hold?**
Hold at least 10 percent of the finishing budget for a well-measured project with a fixed specification. Hold more if the design is still moving or if you are buying in cedis against dollar-priced goods.

**Does the finishing budget include installation?**
Only if you add it. Material quotations, including Decoropic's, are for goods. Installation labour is a separate line, and the installation support Decoropic provides is supervision of local trades, not a replacement for them.
