# China to Southeast Asia: Hotel & Interior Procurement Checklist

A hotel refurbishment in Malaysia, a resort in the Philippines and an apartment project in Vietnam may buy from the same Chinese factories. Their receiving requirements can still be different. Start with the site, your material schedule and the person responsible for importing the goods. Then build a sourcing package around those requirements.

Decoropic provides remote design and material selection, China-side sourcing, quality control, consolidation and export. Your local importer and contractor arrange customs clearance, duties, inland delivery and installation. Our published delivered hotel cases are in Ghana; they show our project experience, not a Southeast Asian office or installation team.

## 1. Turn the project into an orderable brief

Send the destination country and city, project type, room or unit count, current drawings, material quantities, budget range and target delivery date. A drawing alone does not say how many identical rooms will be ordered, which finishes are approved or whether the site can receive a full container.

For a hotel, separate guestroom, bathroom and public-area schedules. For an apartment development, identify the repeated unit types and any show-unit exceptions. For a villa, list the spaces and note items that need field measurements before manufacture.

Name the person who approves samples and the contractor who will install the package. Unclear approval ownership can hold production even when the supplier is ready.

For repeated guestrooms, use our [hotel FF&E RFQ template for Southeast Asia](/guides/hotel-ffe-rfq-template-southeast-asia) to organise item codes, room quantities, specification revisions and quotation exclusions before asking suppliers to price the package.

## 2. Compare packages against the same specification

Price comparisons are only useful when the material, dimensions, hardware, finish and included accessories match. Write these into the request rather than asking several suppliers for their best price on a photograph.

- Furniture: dimensions, core material, exposed finish, hardware, upholstery and assembly method.
- Tiles and slabs: size, finish, approved sample, quantities by area and packing requirements.
- Bathroom systems: vanity dimensions, basin and tap arrangement, mounting details and connections supplied by the installer.
- Lighting: fixture schedule and electrical requirements supplied by the local designer or contractor.
- Doors and windows: opening dimensions, configuration, hardware and approved installation details.

Ask for exclusions as well as inclusions. Accessories, fixing components and spare parts are easy to overlook when the main item looks complete in a product image.

## 3. Confirm destination requirements before production

Use your local customs broker to review each category against the proposed product classification, material composition and country of origin. Check whether documents, permits or product approvals are required before committing to production. A mixed interior package should be reviewed item by item.

The [ASEAN Trade Repository](https://atr.asean.org/) provides access to information on tariffs, origin rules, non-tariff measures and trade procedures. Its [national repository directory](https://atr.asean.org/read/national-trade-repositories-asean-member-countries/60) is a starting point for destination-specific research. Verify the applicable requirements with the destination authority and your broker; this guide does not publish a universal duty rate or claim that all China-sourced materials qualify for a preferential rate.

For the Philippines, the [Bureau of Customs importation guidelines](https://customs.gov.ph/guidelines-on-importation/) describe common import documents and the need to determine whether goods require permits or clearances. Have your importer confirm the documents for the actual goods in your order.

## 4. Approve samples and record the decision

An approved sample should be linked to a written specification and the quantity it represents. Record the finish reference, dimensions, date and approving person. Keep the factory, procurement team and installer working from the same revision.

For repeated guestrooms, resolve the mock-up room before releasing the repeated batch. Check furniture clearances, lighting positions and bathroom interfaces with your local installer. Changes made after a batch is produced affect more than one room.

Photographs help document colour and workmanship, but they do not replace the material specification or measurements. Where appearance is critical, agree how physical samples will be approved and retained.

## 5. Make inspection measurable

Agree the inspection scope before production: what will be checked, how the sample will be selected, which defects need correction and who releases shipment. Useful checks include item identity, dimensions, finish, quantity, visible damage, hardware function and completeness of accessories.

Document findings against item codes in the material schedule. Ask for photographs that connect the inspected goods to their labels and packing, rather than a folder of attractive finished-room pictures. Resolve outstanding issues before goods leave the factory or consolidation point.

See our [China export QC and consolidation checklist](/guides/china-export-qc-consolidation-checklist) for the broader workflow.

## 6. Pack for the receiving sequence

Consolidating multiple categories is useful only if the site can identify, unload and store them. Label cartons by item code and, where appropriate, room type or installation zone. Provide the receiving team with a packing list that matches those labels.

Discuss protection for fragile items, heavy components and finishes that can be damaged by movement. Check the agreed handling method with your local contractor. A container arriving before storage or access is ready can create costs even when production was on time.

Work backwards from the required site date, allowing separate time for sample approval, manufacturing, corrective work, consolidation, freight and local clearance. We confirm a programme for the specific package rather than promising one transit time across Southeast Asia.

## 7. Name the importer, port and final site

The port is not always the final delivery destination. Include the island or inland city where the goods will be installed and identify who owns each local delivery step. This is particularly useful when a project has an additional transfer after the main port.

Keep responsibilities explicit: China-side supply and export, destination import and clearance, inland delivery, unloading, storage and installation. Agree the export terms and payment arrangements in the project quotation. Do not assume a material quote includes all destination costs.

## 8. Send a brief that can be reviewed

Use this format for your first inquiry:

```text
Rooms or units:
Material categories:

```
