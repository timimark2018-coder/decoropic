# How to Ship Furniture from China to Ghana: A Homeowner's Guide

If you plan to ship furniture from China to Ghana, the factory price is only the start. How the pieces are packed, which container option you choose, which Incoterm appears on the invoice and who checks the goods before loading will decide whether your sofa arrives in Accra or Kumasi looking the way it did in the showroom. This guide walks through each stage in plain language, so you can ask the right questions before you pay a deposit.

## The journey at a glance

Most furniture bought in Guangdong leaves from Shenzhen or Guangzhou (Nansha) and arrives at Tema, Ghana's main container port. Published forwarder schedules show sea transit on this lane of roughly four to six weeks port to port, and they note that schedules change with transhipment and port congestion. Production, inspection, clearance and delivery to site add time on either side.

| Stage | Where | Who usually handles it | What can go wrong |
|---|---|---|---|
| Production | Factory, Guangdong | Factory | Wrong finish, size or fabric |
| Inspection and packing | Factory or consolidation warehouse | Buyer's agent or inspector | Damage hidden by poor packing |
| Export clearance and loading | Shenzhen / Nansha | Forwarder | Missing documents, rolled bookings |
| Ocean transit | At sea | Shipping line | Moisture, crushing, delay |
| Import clearance | Tema, via ICUMS | Licensed clearing agent | Declaration errors, demurrage |
| Haulage and installation | Tema to site | Haulier, installer | Breakage when unloading, missing fixings |

Ghana's customs declarations are processed through ICUMS, the Ghana Revenue Authority's single-window system introduced in 2020. We cover the clearance stage in detail in our guide to [Tema port clearing for building materials](/guides/tema-port-clearing-building-materials), so this guide focuses on everything before and after it.

## Packing standards for furniture

Furniture is bulky, fragile at the corners and sensitive to humidity. A container crossing the equator can see large temperature swings, and condensation is a common cause of mould on fabric and swelling on board-based furniture. Ask the seller how each type of item will be packed, and ask to see photos before the boxes are sealed.

| Item type | Minimum packing to ask for | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Upholstered sofas and chairs | Plastic film, foam on arms and legs, corrugated carton or wooden frame | Fabric soiling, torn corners |
| Cabinets and wardrobes (flat-pack) | Foam-lined cartons, corner protectors, labelled fitting bags | Chipped edges, missing hardware |
| Assembled case goods | Foam, cardboard corner guards, wooden crate for large pieces | Crushing under other cargo |
| Stone or glass tops | Wooden crate, packed on edge, separate from the base | Cracking from point loads |
| Mirrors and glass doors | Crate with "this way up" marking | Shattering |
| Mattresses | Heavy film, roll-packed or flat in a carton | Moisture, compression |

Good packing also means good labelling: each carton should show the room, item code and box number (for example "Bedroom 2 – wardrobe – box 3 of 5"). That makes unloading and checking at site far faster.

## FCL, LCL or consolidated container?

There are three common ways to get furniture into a container.

**FCL (full container load).** You book a whole container. It is sealed at origin and opened only at destination, which means less handling and less risk of damage. It makes sense when you have enough volume to fill most of a container.

**LCL (less than container load).** Your cargo shares a container with other shippers' goods. You pay for the space you use, but the cargo is handled more often, at the origin consolidation station and again at the destination, and clearance can take longer while the full container is unstuffed.

**Consolidated by your supplier.** Here one supplier gathers goods from several factories into its own warehouse, checks them, and loads them into one container for you. You get FCL-level handling with items from many sources. Our guide to [container consolidation for house finishing in Ghana](/guides/container-consolidation-house-finishing-ghana) shows how a whole house is planned and packed this way.

Published internal specifications for standard dry containers are below. Real loading capacity for furniture is lower than the nominal volume because pieces are irregular and need protective packing.

| Container | Internal length | Internal width | Internal height | Nominal capacity |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 20GP (20 ft standard) | 5.898 m | 2.352 m | 2.393 m | about 33 m³ |
| 40GP (40 ft standard) | 12.03 m | 2.35 m | 2.39 m | about 67.5 m³ |
| 40HQ (40 ft high cube) | 12.032 m | 2.352 m | 2.698 m | about 76.4 m³ |

Figures vary slightly between carriers; confirm with the line's specification sheet before planning a load.

## Incoterms explained for a homeowner

Incoterms are standard trade terms published by the International Chamber of Commerce. They say where the seller's responsibility ends and yours begins. The three you will see most often:

| Term | What the seller covers | Where risk passes to you | What you still arrange |
|---|---|---|---|
| FOB (Free on Board) | Goods delivered on board the vessel at the Chinese port, export cleared | When goods are on board at origin | Ocean freight, insurance, Tema clearance, duties, delivery |
| CIF (Cost, Insurance and Freight) | FOB plus ocean freight to Tema and minimum cargo insurance | When goods are on board at origin | Tema clearance, duties, delivery |
| DDP (Delivered Duty Paid) | Everything up to the named place in Ghana, import cleared | At the named destination | Unloading and installation, unless agreed |

Two points catch people out. First, under CIF the seller pays freight to Tema, but the risk passes to you once the goods are loaded in China, so if something is damaged at sea you claim on the insurance policy rather than against the seller. Second, the minimum insurance required under CIF is limited cover (Institute Cargo Clauses C, at 110% of the contract value), which does not cover many kinds of handling damage. For furniture, ask whether broader cover is available.

## Preventing damage

Most furniture damage is preventable. The main causes are weak packing, poor stacking in the container and rough unloading. Practical steps:

- **Inspect before packing, not after.** Once a carton is sealed, a scratched door or wrong fabric is hidden until Ghana.
- **Plan the load.** Heavy items go on the floor; upholstery and glass on top or crated; cartons are strapped or braced so they cannot shift.
- **Use desiccants** in containers carrying fabric, timber or board furniture.
- **Photograph the loading** door by door, and record the container and seal numbers.
- **Unload carefully** at site, with enough hands and a clear space, and check carton counts against the packing list before the truck leaves.

## Insurance: what to ask

Marine cargo insurance is priced as a percentage of the insured value and depends on the cover level, the goods and the route; exact premiums are quoted per shipment. When reviewing a policy, ask:

- Is it "all risks" (Institute Cargo Clauses A) or limited cover (Clauses C)?
- Does it run warehouse to warehouse, or only port to port?
- What excess applies, and which exclusions cover packing and gradual moisture damage?
- What evidence is needed for a claim? Usually: photos at loading, photos at unloading, the survey report and the packing list.

## What a freight forwarder does not do

A freight forwarder moves boxes. That is valuable, but many buyers assume more is included than is. Typically a forwarder does not:

- help you choose finishes, sizes or layouts that suit your rooms;
- check that the factory made what you ordered before it is packed;
- consolidate goods from different factories with item-level checks;
- coordinate the order in which items arrive with your builder's programme;
- supervise installation or deal with a missing hinge after delivery.

If those gaps are filled by no one, problems surface on site, when they are most expensive to fix.

## FAQ

**How long does it take to ship furniture from China to Ghana?**
Sea transit from Shenzhen or Nansha to Tema is published at roughly four to six weeks port to port. Add production, inspection, clearance and delivery time; we give a project-specific schedule with each quote.

**Is it cheaper to buy furniture in China than in Ghana?**
It can be, especially for full rooms or whole houses, but the landed cost includes freight, insurance, clearance, duties and delivery. Compare landed cost, not factory price.

**Should I choose FOB or CIF?**
If you have a trusted forwarder and want control of the shipping, FOB. If you prefer the seller to arrange freight to Tema, CIF. In both cases, check the insurance level.

**Can I ship one sofa?**
Yes, through LCL or by adding it to a consolidated container. Small shipments cost more per cubic metre, so it often helps to combine furniture with other items for the same project.

**Who installs the furniture in Ghana?**
Factories and forwarders usually do not. Decoropic supervises installation in Ghana for projects we supply.
