# How Long to Finish a House in Ghana? A Stage-by-Stage Timeline

How long to finish a house in Ghana depends less on the trades and more on the supply chain feeding them. Once the structure is weather-tight, the finishing stage is a sequence of dependencies: measurement cannot happen before plastering, cabinets cannot be made before measurement, and installation cannot start until the container has cleared Tema. This guide lays out that sequence for a project supplied from China, gives indicative durations for each stage, shows what runs in parallel, and identifies the decisions that most often add weeks.

## The critical path

For a house finished with imported joinery, doors and windows, sanitaryware and lighting, the critical path runs through production and shipping. Nothing on site can shorten the time a container spends at sea. The practical consequence is that the finishing clock starts at site measurement, and every week of delay in design approval adds a week to handover.

## Stage-by-stage timeline

The durations below are indicative for a single detached house. Multi-unit developments follow the same sequence with longer production and more containers.

| Stage | What happens | Indicative duration | Depends on |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0. Structure ready | Roof on, external render done, internal plaster complete, first-fix services in | Set by the contractor | Construction programme |
| 1. Site measurement | Every room, opening and wet area measured and photographed | 1 to 2 days on site | Plaster complete |
| 2. Design and specification | Tier chosen per category; drawings, hardware schedule, itemised quotation | 1 to 3 weeks | Client decision speed |
| 3. Order and factory drawings | Deposit or L/C; shop drawings approved | 1 week | Signed specification |
| 4. Production | Cabinets, wardrobes, doors, windows made; sanitaryware, lighting, furniture called off | Confirmed with your quote | Approved drawings; factory holidays |
| 5. QC, packing and consolidation | Inspection against specification; packing; loading at the Foshan warehouse | 1 to 2 weeks | All categories complete |
| 6. Export formalities and sailing | Booking, customs export, vessel departure | Within the consolidation window | Vessel schedule |
| 7. Sea transit | Shenzhen or Guangzhou to Tema | About 30 days port to port; forwarders quote 32 to 42 days for full containers | Carrier routing |
| 8. Clearance at Tema | ICUMS declaration, duty payment, release | Confirmed with your quote for a clean file | Documents and funds ready |
| 9. Delivery to site | Haulage, unloading, count against packing list | 1 to 3 days | Site access and storage |
| 10. Installation | Windows and doors, then tiling, then kitchens and wardrobes, then sanitaryware, then lighting | 4 to 8 weeks for a 4 bedroom house — confirmed with your quote | Trade availability; wet trades complete |
| 11. Snagging and handover | Defects list, corrections, sign-off | 1 to 2 weeks | Installation complete |

Adding the stages gives a finishing duration of several months from measurement to handover, with production and sea transit the two largest blocks. Do not quote a total to a family or a lender until the production lead time for your specific order is confirmed.

## What runs in parallel

Good sequencing recovers weeks.

- **Local purchases during production.** Standard tiles, cement, POP, paint and electrical consumables are bought in Ghana while the container is being made and shipped. Tiles are now manufactured locally, so tiling can be completed before the container arrives, provided wet-area dimensions were fixed at measurement.
- **Ceilings and first coat of paint** before the container lands, so joinery goes into finished rooms.
- **Electrical and plumbing second-fix preparation.** Cables pulled to lighting positions, pipework pressure-tested, so that fittings can be connected the day they arrive.
- **Clearance documents** prepared and lodged while the vessel is at sea.

## The installation sequence on site

Order matters. Installing in the wrong sequence damages finished work.

1. External doors and windows, so the house is secure and dry
2. Internal door frames
3. Floor and wall tiling, waterproofing in wet areas
4. Ceilings and first paint coat
5. Kitchen and wardrobe carcasses, then worktops, then doors and fronts
6. Internal door leaves and ironmongery
7. Sanitaryware and brassware
8. Lighting fittings, switches and sockets
9. Final paint coat and skirting
10. Loose furniture and soft furnishings

## Decisions that add weeks

| Decision delayed | Typical cost in time |
|---|---|
| Choosing the tier late, or changing it after drawings | Redrawing and requoting: 1 to 2 weeks |
| Appliance models not confirmed before kitchen drawings | Drawing revision, or a remake if the cut-out is wrong |
| Measuring from drawings instead of on site | Remake of affected items plus a second shipment |
| Shipping before all categories are ready | A second container, or LCL for the remainder, with its own clearance |
| Documents not ready at arrival | Days of port storage and demurrage |
| Ordering around Chinese New Year or the October holiday | Factory closure of one to two weeks plus a backlog |

## Managing the programme with the contractor

The finishing supplier and the building contractor work to different clocks, and the handover suffers when neither owns the interface. Agree three things in writing at the start: the date plaster will be complete and measurement can happen, the condition of the house on the day the container arrives (dry, lockable, floors tiled, ceilings and first paint done), and which trades belong to whom. Hold a short weekly call during production and shipping so the contractor knows the arrival date is moving as the vessel schedule firms up, and so the supplier knows whether the site will be ready.

## Planning backwards from a move-in date

If the household has a target date, work backwards: subtract snagging, installation, delivery, clearance, sea transit, consolidation and production from the date, and the result is the latest day on which the signed specification must be in hand. Then subtract the design period and you have the latest date for site measurement, which in turn tells the contractor when plastering must be complete. Sharing this reverse schedule with the contractor at the start aligns the structural programme with the finishing programme.

## FAQ

**What is the fastest realistic finishing timeline?**
The floor is set by production plus about 30 days at sea plus clearance. Fast projects are those where the specification is signed quickly, appliances are confirmed early and documents are ready before the vessel arrives.

**Can I ship in two containers to start installation sooner?**
Yes. Doors, windows and sanitaryware can go first so that the house is secured and wet areas finished, with kitchens, wardrobes, lighting and furniture following. Each container carries its own freight and clearance costs.

**Does the rainy season affect the timeline?**
Heavy rain affects external work and site access more than internal finishing. Keep deliveries off the wettest weeks where possible and make sure the storage room is dry.

**How long does installation take for a 4 bedroom house?**
With windows, doors, two or three joinery rooms, four or five bathrooms and full lighting, allow several weeks with supervised trades. The exact figure depends on how much tiling and painting was completed before the container arrived.
