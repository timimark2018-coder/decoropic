# Bathroom Fittings Suppliers in Ghana: What to Import and What to Buy Locally

Comparing bathroom fittings suppliers in Ghana comes down to one question per item: is this better imported as part of a matched set, or bought locally where it can be replaced in an afternoon? A bathroom combines ceramics, brassware, glass, cabinetry and the plumbing that connects them, and the right answer differs for each. This guide splits a Ghana bathroom into its parts, explains the water-pressure and fitting-standard issues that catch importers out, and sets out what a workable warranty and spares arrangement looks like for a house in Accra, Tema or Kumasi.

The pattern is clear: import the visible, matched, hard-to-source items as a set, and buy the pipework, consumables and anything that may need same-day replacement locally.

## Water pressure: the issue that breaks imported showers

Many Ghanaian houses are fed by a roof tank or a ground tank with a booster pump. Pressure at the shower therefore depends on tank height or pump setting, not on a utility main.

- **Gravity-fed from a roof tank** gives low pressure. Thermostatic mixers and rain showers specified for pressurised systems may perform poorly. Specify low-pressure-rated mixers, or add a booster pump.
- **Pump-fed systems** can give high and fluctuating pressure. Specify fittings rated for the pump's maximum and fit a pressure-reducing valve if the pump is oversized.
- **Hot and cold balance.** If hot water comes from an instantaneous heater at low pressure and cold from a pump, a mixer will struggle. Balance the two supplies or use a heater that suits the system.

Record the water supply arrangement at measurement stage and give it to the supplier with the bathroom specification. It is far cheaper to specify the right cartridge than to replace forty mixers.

## Fitting standards and compatibility

Imported sanitaryware must connect to local plumbing. Check these before ordering.

| Check | What to confirm |
|---|---|
| Pipe thread standard | Local plumbing in Ghana generally uses BSP-threaded fittings; confirm imported brassware and flexible connectors are BSP, not NPT |
| Waste outlet sizes | Basin and bath wastes, WC outlet position and diameter, floor drain outlet matched to the local waste pipe |
| WC outlet type | Horizontal (P-trap) or vertical (S-trap) to match the drainage already cast into the slab; rough-in distance from the wall |
| Wall-hung WC frame | Wall build-up and stud depth; the frame must be ordered with the pan |
| Angle valves and connectors | Supply with the set; local sizes may differ |
| Water heater connections | Inlet and outlet sizes; electrical rating at 230 V, 50 Hz |
| Shower tray and drain | Tray outlet position relative to the floor drain; tile thickness for the drain height |

Ask the plumber to confirm the drainage positions on site, with photos and dimensions, before the sanitaryware is drawn. The WC rough-in is the most common mismatch.

## Warranty and spares

A warranty on imported sanitaryware is only useful if parts can reach you. Agree the following with the supplier at order:

- **Spares kit shipped with the order**: mixer cartridges, flush valves and fill valves for the cisterns, seals, shower hoses, soft-close seat hinges. One set per five to ten bathrooms is a common ratio.
- **Who diagnoses a fault**: the supplier's technician at installation, then a nominated local plumber.
- **How replacement parts travel**: with the next container, or by courier for small items.
- **What is excluded**: damage from debris in the pipework, incorrect pressure, or installation not supervised by the supplier.

Flush the pipework before connecting any imported brassware. Construction debris in the lines is the most common cause of early cartridge failure and is not a product defect.

## FAQ

**Can I import just the taps and showers and buy the ceramics locally?**
Yes. Brassware is where imported quality shows most. If you do this, make sure the basin and bath tap holes and the shower rough-in match the imported fittings before the plumber starts.

**Does Ghana require any certificate for sanitaryware?**
The Ghana Standards Authority lists used sanitary ware among prohibited used goods under its conformity programme; new sanitaryware is not named in the broad groups published, but confirm current coverage of your HS codes with your clearing agent before shipment.

**What finish lasts longest near the coast?**
PVD finishes outperform standard chrome plating in salt air. Chrome remains acceptable inland and in Functional-tier bathrooms with regular cleaning.

**Should the shower enclosure come from China or be made locally?**
Frameless enclosures with matched hardware are an import item and must be measured after tiling. Simple framed screens can be made by local glaziers to a good standard.
