# Importing Lighting into Ghana: Electrical Standards, Conformity and LED Driver Quality

Lighting is the easiest category to get wrong on a Ghana import order. Fittings designed for other voltages, drivers that fail in heat, sockets that do not match the Ghanaian wiring tradition and products without the required conformity evidence all end up as callbacks or, worse, as fire risks. This guide sets out the supply characteristics, plug and socket conventions, the role of the Ghana Standards Authority, what makes an LED driver survive Ghanaian conditions and how to prepare a lighting schedule for import.

## The supply: 230 V, 50 Hz, Type G

Ghana's domestic supply is nominally 230 V at 50 Hz, and the standard socket and plug is the British-type three-pin Type G (BS 1363 pattern) with a rectangular earth pin. These conventions come from the British wiring tradition and remain the basis of Ghanaian domestic installations.

| Parameter | Ghana convention | Implication for imports |
|---|---|---|
| Voltage | 230 V nominal | Specify 220–240 V rated drivers and lamps; 110–120 V products will fail |
| Frequency | 50 Hz | Most LED drivers accept 50/60 Hz; confirm on the label |
| Plug and socket | Type G, three-pin, fused plug | Table and floor lamps should ship with Type G plugs, not adapters |
| Wiring practice | British-pattern ring and radial circuits, earthing | Fittings must have a proper earth terminal unless Class II double-insulated |
| Supply quality | Voltage fluctuation and outages occur | Drivers with wide input range and surge tolerance last longer |

## Ghana Standards Authority and conformity

The Ghana Standards Authority (GSA) is the national standards body and has publicly stated that it wants only certified electrical products on the market, linking substandard cables, sockets and fittings to fire outbreaks. Importers of electrical goods should expect their consignments to be subject to conformity checks at destination, and dealers are urged to stock certified products only.

What that means in practice for a lighting order:

- Ask the factory for the product's test reports and certificates (for example IEC-based safety test reports for luminaires and drivers, and CE or equivalent documentation) and keep them with the shipment.
- Confirm with your clearing agent the current documentary and inspection requirements for electrical items at Tema before shipment.
- Avoid unbranded drivers and cables with no markings; they are the items most often rejected or found substandard.

This guide does not state the detailed inspection procedure because it changes; your clearing agent and the GSA's own published guidance are the authoritative sources.

## LED drivers: the component that fails first

An LED fitting is only as good as its driver. In Ghana three stresses combine: ambient heat in enclosed ceilings, voltage fluctuation, and long operating hours. Specification points:

| Driver feature | Why it matters in Ghana |
|---|---|
| Wide input range (for example 170–264 V or wider) | Tolerates brown-outs and spikes better than a narrow 220–240 V driver |
| Surge protection built in | Generator switchovers and grid faults produce transients |
| Thermal rating and derating curve | Ceiling voids in Accra can exceed 40 °C; drivers rated for high case temperature last longer |
| Replaceable driver (not potted into the fitting) | A failed driver can be swapped instead of the whole downlight |
| Flicker-free constant-current output | Comfort and video quality; cheap drivers flicker at low voltage |
| Recognised component brands or test reports | Easier to evidence conformity |

Ask for a spare-driver allowance of a few per cent on any hotel or multi-unit order.

## Colour temperature, output and IP ratings

- **Colour temperature.** 3000 K (warm white) for living areas and hotels; 4000 K for kitchens and offices. Mixing temperatures in one room looks poor; set a schedule and stick to it.
- **Output.** Specify lumens, not watts. A living room typically needs a lower level than a kitchen worktop.
- **Colour rendering.** CRI 80 minimum, 90 for retail and show units.
- **IP ratings.** IP44 for bathrooms outside the shower zone, IP65 for shower zones and external fittings, and corrosion-resistant bodies on coastal sites.
- **Dimming.** Only pair dimmable drivers with compatible dimmers; mixing leads to flicker.

## Preparing a lighting schedule for import

A lighting schedule lists every fitting by room with its type, quantity, lumen output, colour temperature, IP rating, driver type and mounting detail. Add:

- Ceiling construction (concrete slab, plasterboard, timber) so the right cut-out and fixing kit is supplied
- Switch positions and circuits, so two-way switching and dimming are planned
- Emergency and external lighting requirements for hotels and apartment blocks
- Spares: lamps and drivers

Send the schedule with the plan drawings. The factory then supplies fittings, drivers and accessories as one labelled package per room, which travels in the same container as the joinery.

## Installation in Ghana

All installation should be by a licensed electrician to local practice. Points to brief:

- Earth every Class I fitting; test the installation
- Keep driver ventilation space in ceiling voids
- Use the correct cable sizes and terminations; no twisted joints
- Label circuits at the consumer unit
- Commission by switching every circuit and checking for flicker and buzz

## Generators, inverters and solar

Many homes and most hotels in Ghana run a standby generator, and inverter and solar systems are increasingly common. Lighting should be planned with this in mind. LED fittings draw little, so putting all lighting on the inverter or essential circuit is practical and keeps the house usable during an outage. Generator changeovers produce voltage dips and spikes, which is one more reason to specify drivers with a wide input range and surge tolerance. If the house has a solar inverter, confirm the inverter's output waveform is suitable for electronic drivers and dimmers; cheap modified-sine-wave inverters cause flicker and early driver failure.

## Fittings for Ghanaian room types

A few room-specific notes from installations in Accra and Kumasi: recessed downlights need a ceiling void, so in rooms with a concrete slab and no false ceiling use surface-mounted or pendant fittings; verandas and car ports need IP65 fittings with insect-resistant seals; external wall lights on coastal sites should be aluminium or stainless with marine coating; and staircases benefit from low-level step lights on the essential circuit. In hotels, corridors and stairs should have emergency luminaires with battery back-up tested monthly.

## FAQ

**Can I use US-spec 120 V fittings with a transformer?**
Not advisable. Specify 230 V products; transformers add cost, heat and a point of failure.

**Do fittings need a specific mark for Ghana?**
Conformity evidence is expected for electrical products; confirm the current requirement with your clearing agent and the GSA before shipping.

**What colour temperature is best for a rental apartment?**
3000 K throughout living and bedrooms, 4000 K in the kitchen.

**Why do my LED downlights fail after a year?**
Most often a driver overheating in the ceiling void or failing on voltage spikes. Replaceable, wide-range drivers fix both.
