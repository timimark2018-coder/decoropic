# Building a House in Ghana from Abroad: How to Pay by Milestones and Keep Control

Building a house in Ghana from abroad puts you in a position every diaspora builder recognises: you are funding a project you cannot visit, through people you cannot watch, in a market where materials are easy to divert. The structure can be managed with a reputable contractor and a quantity surveyor. The finishing stage, where the money per square metre is highest and the number of suppliers multiplies, is where control is most often lost. This guide sets out a milestone-based approach to paying for finishing, the evidence to demand at each step, and why consolidating under one accountable supplier reduces risk.

## Where diaspora projects go wrong at finishing stage

The common failure pattern is not outright fraud. It is drift.

- Cash is sent for "tiles and cement" and spent partly on tiles, partly on the previous week's labour shortfall.
- Materials are bought in small lots from several shops, with handwritten receipts that cannot be reconciled.
- A relative or site caretaker is asked to supervise trades they do not understand.
- Specification quietly drops: the quoted branded hinges become unbranded, the porcelain becomes ceramic.
- Nobody owns the problem when the kitchen does not fit, because the carpenter blames the mason and the mason blames the drawing.

Each of these is a payment-structure problem before it is a people problem. If funds move only against defined deliverables with evidence, drift has nowhere to go.

## The milestone principle

Pay for outcomes you can verify, not for time or promises. For imported finishing materials the outcomes are naturally sequential, which makes them easy to tie to payments.

| Milestone | What has been delivered | Evidence you should receive | Typical payment trigger |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Measurement complete | Site measured after plastering; measurement sheets issued | Photos of each room with tape visible; dimensioned sketch | Design fee or deposit, if any |
| 2. Design and specification signed off | Drawings, hardware schedule, tier selection, itemised quotation | PDF drawings and schedule you approve in writing | Deposit against order — confirmed with your quote |
| 3. Production complete and inspected | Goods made, QC report against the specification | Dated photos and video of goods in the factory or warehouse, with your order number visible | Balance before shipment, or L/C documents |
| 4. Shipped | Container sealed and on the vessel | Bill of lading, packing list, container and seal numbers, loading photos | Shipping and clearance costs |
| 5. Cleared and delivered | Goods at your site, counted against the packing list | Delivery note signed by your representative; photos of unloading | Clearance and delivery balance |
| 6. Installed and signed off | Installation checked against the drawing | Room-by-room photos; snag list closed | Installation supervision balance |

The exact percentages at each stage are a commercial matter between you and the supplier; the principle is that no payment is made without the evidence in the next column.

## Evidence standards: what a good photo set looks like

Photos protect both sides. Set the standard before the first payment.

- **Timestamped and geotagged** where the phone allows; in Ghana, a GhanaPost GPS digital address for the site (the format is region and district letters, an area code, and a unique code for a 5 m by 5 m square) removes any doubt about location.
- **Reference visible.** A printed order number or your name on a card in the frame of factory and warehouse photos.
- **Wide and close.** One wide shot establishing context, then close-ups of the detail that matters: the edge banding, the hinge brand, the tile batch code.
- **Video walk-through** of the container being loaded, showing the door being closed and the seal applied, with the seal number readable.
- **Delivery count** on video, with the packing list in hand.

Ask for these in a shared folder, not scattered across WhatsApp chats, so you can find the evidence months later if a warranty question arises.

## Preventing material diversion

Material diversion is the quiet loss of goods between purchase and installation. Imported, consolidated supply reduces it in three ways.

1. **One packing list.** Every item in the container is listed with quantity. What is unloaded can be counted against it on the day.
2. **Project-specific goods.** A kitchen made to your measurements, wardrobes cut to your ceiling height and doors sized to your openings have little resale value on another site. Generic goods such as cement and standard tiles are far easier to divert, which is a further reason to buy those locally in small, scheduled lots.
3. **Fewer hands.** A single supplier that measures, makes, ships and supervises installation means fewer transfers of custody.

On site, basic controls still apply: a lockable store, a materials register, and installation scheduled so goods arrive shortly before they are fitted rather than sitting for months.

## Why a single accountable supplier matters

When finishing materials come from eight suppliers and three countries, the gaps between them are where problems live. Nobody measured for the door frames because the door supplier assumed the window supplier had. The tap does not fit the basin because they were bought separately. A single supplier taking responsibility from measurement to installation closes those gaps and, importantly, gives you one party to hold to the specification.

What to require of that supplier:

- A physical presence in Ghana you can verify, with an address and a named contact
- Measurement done by their own staff on your site
- Itemised quotation with brand or grade named for every line
- Written QC and loading evidence before the balance is paid
- Payment through bank transfer (T/T) or letter of credit, never cash to individuals
- A clear statement of what installation support includes

## Managing from abroad: a practical routine

| Frequency | Action |
|---|---|
| Weekly during design | Review drawings; approve or comment in writing |
| At each milestone | Check evidence against the table above before releasing payment |
| Monthly during build | Video call from site with your representative walking the rooms |
| At delivery | Have a trusted person count against the packing list on camera |
| At installation | Request room-by-room photos; raise a snag list; sign off in writing |

Keep one folder with the drawings, quotation, payment receipts, bill of lading, packing list and photo sets. That folder is your protection if anything is disputed later.

## FAQ

**Can I pay everything through a letter of credit?**
An L/C is well suited to the goods portion of the order, with shipping documents as the release trigger. Services performed in Ghana, such as measurement and installation supervision, are normally paid by bank transfer against invoice.

**Who checks the goods at the factory if I am not there?**
The supplier's QC team should inspect against the specification and send dated photos and video. If you want independent verification, third-party inspection companies operate in Guangdong and can be appointed for a fee.

**What if the local contractor refuses to work with imported materials?**
Agree the scope split at the start: structure and wet trades to the contractor, imported finishing to the supplier, with the supplier's technician supervising fitting. Most contractors accept this when it is in the contract from day one.

**How do I avoid paying duty twice or overpaying at Tema?**
Use a licensed clearing agent and insist on the ICUMS tax bill as your record. Duties and levies are assessed on the declared value, so the commercial invoice and payment records must be consistent. See the Tema clearing guide for the document list.
